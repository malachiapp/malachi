Malachi – messages straight from device to device

A free messenger for Android. No phone number, no account and no server your messages pass through. End-to-end encrypted, with Tor built in.

Get the Android app How it works

At a glance

  • No account, no number

    You don't sign up anywhere. You add contacts by exchanging an invitation – ideally in person via QR code, otherwise as a link or code. Or you both enter your phone numbers or an agreed identifier; your devices use them to calculate a meeting point in the Tor network, and none of it is stored or sent.

  • No Malachi server

    There is no server that stores or relays your messages. Messages, files and calls travel between your devices – via Tor first, then directly whenever possible.

  • End-to-end encrypted

    Every message gets its own key (Double Ratchet). Every connection negotiates a secret with ML-KEM-768, a scheme designed to resist future quantum computers; the Double Ratchet combines it with X25519. Voice and video calls are end-to-end encrypted, but not yet protected against future quantum computers.

  • Everything a messenger needs

    One-to-one and group chats, channels with up to 250 subscribers, voice messages, pictures and files, voice and video calls – all encrypted. Group members who don't know each other yet can connect through the group – only if both agree.

  • Seal mode

    Encrypt texts and files for WhatsApp, Signal and others. The other messenger only carries ciphertext. Encrypting is part of the full version, decrypting is free.

  • Free

    The app is free. If you want more, buy the full version for €2.99 once – no subscription, no account.

How it works – in plain words

  1. Invite. You show your contact a QR code (or send a link). It tells their device how to reach yours over Tor. No phone number or username is needed.
  2. Agree on keys. Your devices negotiate secret keys between themselves. You can compare the safety code (shield icon in the chat) – if it matches, nobody is sitting in between.
  3. Write. Each message is encrypted on your phone and only decrypted again on your contact's phone. On the way it is nothing but ciphertext.
  4. Deliver. If both devices are reachable, the message arrives directly. Otherwise it waits, encrypted, on your device and is delivered automatically as soon as you are connected again. There is no storage on the internet in between.

Who sees what?

Only you and your contact ever see the content. Who can see your IP address depends on the connection mode you choose in the settings:

Honest limits

  • For a message to arrive, both devices have to be reachable at the same time at some point – there is no server that keeps it in the meantime.
  • No encryption helps against malware with full access to the phone. While Malachi is locked, your chat histories are additionally encrypted with a key derived from your app PIN – even if someone holds the unlocked phone. Someone who has the phone and attacks it with special tools can try out a short PIN more easily than a long one. The contact list and your own messages not yet delivered are needed for receiving in the background; they are encrypted only with the device key, not additionally with the PIN.
  • Security updates are provided as long as Malachi is offered – at least until 31 December 2031. The date moves forward by one year every year; we announce an end at least twelve months in advance. Security updates are always free.
  • Encryption hides content, not every trace: with direct connections your contact sees your IP address, and the Tor entry node always sees it (see above). Malachi itself asks no STUN services; only the Tor bridge Snowflake uses its own when Tor is blocked on your network.
  • Without STUN, direct connections work less often on some networks (IPv4 only, mobile data without IPv6, hotel Wi-Fi). Then everything runs over Tor: slower, calls only as voice with delay.
  • Malachi has not been reviewed by an independent auditor yet.
  • Malachi is currently only available for Android (version 7.0 and later).
