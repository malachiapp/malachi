Questions & answers

Basics

How do I find contacts without a phone number?

Not automatically – and that's intentional. Malachi doesn't upload an address book and has no user directories. You exchange an invitation: ideally in person via QR code, otherwise as a link or code over a channel you trust. If you share a link, compare the safety code afterwards (shield icon in the chat) – otherwise someone who intercepts the link on the way could put themselves in between. Alternatively you both enter your phone numbers or an agreed identifier (at least 8 characters); this creates a meeting point in the Tor network, and what you enter is neither stored nor sent.

How do messages arrive if there is no server?

Every device is reachable over Tor as its own onion service. That is how your devices find each other and exchange messages; depending on the connection mode they then switch to a direct connection. If your contact isn't reachable right now, the message waits, encrypted, on your device and goes out automatically as soon as you are connected again.

Who can see my IP address?

That depends on the connection mode (Settings → Connection & Tor). In the default "Direct when possible" mode, your contact sees your IP address – already when your devices try to connect directly. Malachi asks no STUN service and no server for this. With "Maximum anonymity (Tor only)" (full version) neither contacts nor any server see it. Your IP address always reaches the Tor entry node, as with any use of Tor – but it does not know who you are talking to. Only if Tor is blocked on your network and the Snowflake bridge steps in does it ask its own STUN servers run by the Tor Project. The overview is on the home page.

How is Malachi encrypted?

End-to-end: messages are encrypted on your device and only decrypted at your contact's. Every message gets its own key, which is deleted after use (Double Ratchet). If a device is briefly compromised, the connection recovers with the next messages – unless the attacker actively interferes; comparing the safety code protects against that. Every connection negotiates a secret with ML-KEM-768, a standardised scheme intended to protect against future quantum computers as well; the Double Ratchet combines it with the proven X25519. This applies to messages, pictures, files and voice messages. Voice and video calls are end-to-end encrypted (DTLS-SRTP), but not yet protected against future quantum computers.

Is Malachi open source?

Yes. The complete source code is available under the GNU AGPL-3.0 license on GitHub – the app, the website, the shop and the reports of our internal security reviews. You can build the app yourself; your own build is then signed with your own key. Please report vulnerabilities privately, not publicly.

Is Malachi unbreakable?

No – no software can honestly promise that. Malachi builds on proven, publicly known schemes (including X25519, ML-KEM-768 and XChaCha20-Poly1305) instead of home-made ones. However, Malachi has not been reviewed by an independent auditor yet. No encryption helps against malware with full access to the phone.

What is seal mode?

With seal mode you encrypt texts, pictures, voice messages and files that you send over another messenger such as WhatsApp or Signal. The other messenger only sees ciphertext. Seal has its own vault with its own passphrase, its own contacts (paired via QR code) and keeps no history. Encrypting is part of the full version, decrypting is free.

Is Malachi available for iPhone, Windows or in the browser?

Not at the moment. Malachi is only available for Android (version 7.0 and later).

Which languages does the app support?

18: German, English, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian, Arabic, Persian, Chinese (simplified), Japanese, Korean, Hindi and Indonesian.

Download and updates

How do I know the file is genuine?

Compare the SHA-256 checksum and the certificate fingerprint with the values on the download page – ideally over two routes, such as the normal and the onion address. The instructions are on the download page.

How do I get updates?

The app checks for new versions over Tor at most once a day and only downloads one when you tap it; new versions also appear on the download page. Simply install them over the old version; your data is kept. Android only accepts an update if it is signed with the same key as the installed app. Security updates are provided as long as Malachi is offered – at least until 31 December 2031. The date moves forward by one year every year; we announce an end at least twelve months in advance. Security updates are always free.

Why is there an onion address?

With the onion address you reach this website in the Tor Browser without the server or anyone on the network in between learning your IP address. The content is the same on both addresses.

Full version and purchase

What does Malachi cost?

The app is free. The full version costs €2.99 once – no subscription, no account.

Why only Monero and Bitcoin?

So you can buy without an account, without bank or card details and without a name. The shop is only reachable over Tor and only learns a device code.

I have a new phone. Is the full version gone?

No. With the order number and recovery code you get it onto the new device in the shop – up to three times in total. That's why you must write down the recovery code when you buy.

What happens to my chats if I lose my phone?

Your chats are only on your device, not in a cloud and not on a server. Malachi currently offers no backup – without the device they are gone. Someone who finds the phone can't read the histories without your app PIN: they are additionally encrypted with a key derived from the PIN, and voice messages are recorded and played only in memory. Whether you choose 4 or 6 digits is up to you; a longer PIN is harder to guess. With the full version you can also set everything to be deleted after five wrong PINs.
